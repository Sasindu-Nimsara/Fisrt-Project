Bulgaria

Bulgaria is a beautiful country in southeastern Europe, known for its rich history, stunning landscapes, and cultural heritage. From the Black Sea coast to the Balkan Mountains, it offers diverse scenery and ancient landmarks. The capital city, Sofia, blends old-world charm with modern energy, featuring Roman ruins, Orthodox churches, and lively streets.

The country has a deep cultural tradition, including folk music, colorful festivals, and unique cuisine. Bulgarian yogurt and banitsa are popular dishes enjoyed by locals and visitors alike. The Cyrillic alphabet, created in the region, is a major contribution to global culture and literacy.

Nature lovers enjoy Bulgaria’s many hiking trails, ski resorts, and national parks. Whether exploring caves or relaxing at mineral spas, there’s something for everyone. With its friendly people and affordable travel, Bulgaria is a hidden gem waiting to be discovered.

Sofia

Sofia is the capital and largest city of Bulgaria, located at the foot of Vitosha Mountain in the western part of the country. It is one of Europe’s oldest cities, with a history that dates back thousands of years. Today, Sofia is a lively and modern city that blends ancient ruins, historic churches, and Soviet-era buildings with modern shopping centers, cafes, and cultural venues. Key landmarks include the Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, the National Palace of Culture, and the ancient Roman city of Serdica, which lies beneath parts of the modern city. Sofia is also a major educational and economic hub, home to several universities, museums, and theaters. With its mix of history, nature, and urban life, Sofia offers a unique experience for both locals and visitors.

Plovdiv

Plovdiv is the second-largest city in Bulgaria and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Europe. Located in the southern part of the country, it is famous for its rich history, vibrant culture, and charming Old Town. Plovdiv is home to well-preserved Roman ruins, including a stunning ancient amphitheater that is still used for performances today. The city’s cobblestone streets, colorful 19th-century houses, and art galleries make it a favorite destination for tourists and artists alike. In 2019, Plovdiv was named the European Capital of Culture, highlighting its role as a cultural center. With its mix of ancient heritage and modern spirit, Plovdiv offers a unique glimpse into Bulgaria’s past and present.
